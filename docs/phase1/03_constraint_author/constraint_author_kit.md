## §2 — Instructions

You are defining, for each of six fictional portfolio cases, the list of **material constraints** — the
facts a competent professional would need to engage with in order to reason about that position
properly.

### A material constraint IS

- a stated limit (concentration cap, target band, sector cap, liquidity limit)
- a tax or accounting consequence (embedded gain or loss, lot seasoning, a realised-gains budget)
- a liquidity or execution reality (days of average volume required to act)
- a dated event that is stated in the case
- a portfolio-structural fact (concentration, correlation, sector exposure)

**and it must be present in the case description you are given.**

### A material constraint IS NOT

- ✗ any view on what the right action is
- ✗ any directional opinion about the security
- ✗ anything not present in the material you are given
- ✗ anything you think *should* have been provided but was not

### Legitimate policy disagreement

A professional may reasonably reject a stated policy limit — arguing a 15% cap is too tight, for
example. **The existence of that limit is still a material constraint**, because it must be engaged with
in order to be rejected.

**Do not exclude a constraint because you disagree with it.**

### Scope

**Target 3–6 constraints per case.** Fewer than 3 makes the measure insensitive; more than 6 makes
everything look under-engaged. Write each as **one short, checkable clause** — something a reader could
tick or not tick after reading someone's paragraph of reasoning.

### Timing

You are being asked for this **before any participant data exists**. Your list will be frozen and hashed.
It cannot be revised once scoring begins.
