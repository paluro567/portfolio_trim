## §6 — Q1 rubric · Reasoning–action coherence

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **2** | The action follows from the stated reason **and** the magnitude is connected to it |
| **1** | The action follows, but the magnitude is unstated, arbitrary, or unconnected to the reason |
| **0** | The action does not follow from the stated reason, the reason is absent, or reason and action contradict each other |

**Not scored here:** whether you would have chosen the same action.

**Worked examples**
- *"Trim to the cap, staged over two weeks because it's 4 days of volume."* → **2**
- *"Trim — it's oversized."* (no magnitude given, or a number with no link) → **1**
- *"Hold — it's well above the cap."* (reason contradicts action) → **0**

---

## §7 — Q2 rubric · Material-constraint engagement

Tick each constraint from the case's supplied list that the reasoning **explicitly engages** — either
names it, or clearly addresses it in substance.

- A passing mention that has no bearing on the decision **does not count**.
- **Do not tick a constraint the reasoning does not raise, even if you think it should have.**
- Recorded as a **set**, not a score. There is no total to compute.

**Worked examples** *(illustrative constraint list)*
- *"Trim to the cap; I'd stage it, since a full exit is 16 days of volume."* → ticks the cap constraint **and** the liquidity constraint
- *"Trim to the cap."* → ticks the cap constraint only
- *"There's a lot of embedded gain here, so I'd wait for the lots to season."* → ticks the tax/lot constraints, **not** the cap

---

## §8 — Q3 rubric · Claim support

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **2** | No directional assertion beyond what the information supports; stated confidence is proportionate to the basis given |
| **1** | Mild overreach, or confidence somewhat beyond the stated basis |
| **0** | Asserts a directional view the information cannot support, **or** confidence is clearly disproportionate to the basis given |

**A stated personal view labelled as a personal view is NOT an overreach.
An unlabelled assertion presented as fact IS.**

**Worked examples**
- *"I have no view on direction; I'm acting on the size."* → **2**
- *"My own instinct is it's expensive, but that's a hunch."* → **2** *(labelled)*
- *"It's probably due for a pullback, confidence 4."* → **0** *(unlabelled assertion, high confidence)*
- *"The evidence is mixed, so I'm going to trim — it's clearly weakening."* → **0** *(contradicts its own premise)*

## §9 — UNSUP flag instructions

Tick **yes** if either applies:

1. **Assertion not present in the case.** The reasoning relies on a fact, figure, or event that does not
   appear anywhere in the material provided.
2. **Deference without reasoning.** The reasoning defers to an external source in place of reasoning —
   *"because the page says so"*, *"following the recommendation"*, *"it told me to"* — with no reason
   given of the person's own.

Tick **no** if the person gives their own reason, **even if that reason is weak or you disagree with it**.

**UNSUP is independent of Q1, Q2 and Q3.** A record can score Q1 = 2 and still be flagged.

## §10 — Blinding guess field

Every record asks: *"Was this written before or after the person saw additional material?"*

- Answer on impression. **Guessing is expected and there is no penalty for being wrong.**
- Use **can't tell** freely — it is a legitimate and useful answer.
- Your accuracy on this question is measured as a check on the study's own procedure, **not on you.**
